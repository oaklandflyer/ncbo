# Recruiting Table Kit

Most clubs gain the majority of a year's members in about two weeks. Prepare for those.

## What to bring
- [ ] Sign-up sheet — name and email only. Nothing else. Do not collect phone numbers,
      student ID numbers, weight, or "goals" at a table in public
- [ ] A pen that works, and a spare
- [ ] One-page handout: what the club is, when it meets, where, and how to join
- [ ] QR code to the club's page or sign-up form
- [ ] Something to sit behind and something to put it on, if the venue does not provide it
- [ ] Two officers minimum, so the table is never unattended

## What to say
A tabling pitch is two sentences, then a question. Long pitches lose people.

> "We're the bodybuilding and fitness club — we train together, run posing and prep
> sessions, and you don't have to compete to join. Do you lift already, or are you looking
> to start?"

Both answers are good answers. The second one is the person most clubs accidentally scare
off, and the one most likely to still be a member in three years.

## What not to do
- Do not comment on anyone's body. Not as a compliment, not as encouragement, not ever
- Do not promise coaching, results, timelines, or that competing is easy
- Do not give nutrition advice at the table
- Do not imply NCBO runs a season or competition that does not yet exist
- Do not photograph people without asking

## Follow-up
- [ ] Email everyone who signed up within 48 hours, while they remember signing
- [ ] First email says exactly one thing: the next meeting, time and place
- [ ] Anyone who attends once gets a second, personal message. That is the step that converts
- [ ] Keep the sign-up list only as long as you need it, then delete it

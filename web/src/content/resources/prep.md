This page summarises the peer-reviewed recommendations for natural contest preparation. It
is a summary of published research, not a plan, and not advice about you specifically.

## Rate of weight loss

Set calories so that weight loss lands at roughly **0.5–1% of bodyweight per week**. This is
the range the evidence supports for retaining lean mass while getting lean. Faster loss
costs muscle, which is the opposite of the point.

The practical implication is about the calendar, not the kitchen: a prep that starts late
cannot be rescued by cutting harder. If the arithmetic says you need more weeks than you
have, the honest answer is to pick a later show.

## Protein

Within that calorie target, most — though not all — natural bodybuilders respond best to
**2.3–3.1 g/kg of lean body mass per day**.

Note the denominator: **lean body mass**, not total bodyweight. The recommendation shifts
toward the top of the range, and above it, for competitors who are already very lean and for
those running larger deficits — the leaner you get and the harder you diet, the more protein
does to protect muscle.

For general training rather than contest prep, the ISSN position stand puts overall daily
intake at **1.4–2.0 g/kg of bodyweight per day** as sufficient for most exercising people to
build and maintain muscle.

## Fat and carbohydrate

**Fat: 15–30% of calories.** **Carbohydrate: the remainder** once protein and fat are set.

Carbohydrate is what is left over rather than a target of its own, but it is not a leftover
in importance — it is what fuels training hard enough to justify the protein.

## Meal distribution

- **3–6 meals per day.**
- Around training, roughly **0.4–0.5 g/kg of bodyweight of protein** per dose.
- The ISSN position stand suggests **0.25 g/kg of a high-quality protein per dose**, or
  20–40 g absolute, distributed **every 3–4 hours** across the day.

The number of meals matters less than hitting the daily totals and spreading protein sensibly.
Anyone selling you a magic meal count is selling you something.

## Supplements with supporting evidence

Three, and the list is short on purpose:

- **Creatine monohydrate**
- **Caffeine**
- **Beta-alanine**

**Carbohydrate loading** has a theoretical rationale for a bodybuilding context, but it is
**understudied** — the evidence base does not support prescriptive protocols, and it is
usually attempted for the first time in the week when a mistake costs the most.

### Supplement safety — read this part twice

Per the U.S. Anti-Doping Agency, if you use supplements at all:

- Use only **third-party certified** products. USADA recognises **NSF Certified for Sport**
  as meeting the criteria for a third-party certifier. A credible programme is accredited to
  **ISO 17065**, with analysis done in an ISO 17025 laboratory whose scope covers dietary
  supplements.
- **Verify the lot number** on the certifier's site — certification applies to specific lots,
  not to a brand in general.
- Understand what certification buys you: using a certified product **"significantly reduces,
  but does not necessarily eliminate"** the chance of testing positive.

Uncertified supplements have been found to contain undeclared steroids, pharmaceuticals and
research drugs. For a student athlete, "I didn't know it was in there" is not a defence that
protects your eligibility.

## What this page will never contain

These exclusions are policy, not oversight. They are written down in the repository's
content policy so that nobody adds them later by accident.

- **No dehydration, water-cutting, water-loading or electrolyte-manipulation protocols.** Not
  as instructions, not as a description of "what people do", not as a table. The Helms review
  is explicit that this practice **can be dangerous, and may not improve appearance**. That is
  the whole of what this site has to say about it.
- **No diuretics of any kind**, including over-the-counter and "natural" ones.
- **No PED, SARM or pro-hormone dosing, sourcing or cycling information.** What is banned and
  why is a factual matter and appears on the [competing page](/resources/competing); anything
  actionable does not appear anywhere.
- **No individual calorie targets, cut timelines or body-fat targets**, and no goal-weight
  calculators. Those require knowing a specific person, and a website does not.

If you need numbers for *you*, that is a conversation with a registered dietitian and your
campus sports medicine service — see [health and wellbeing](/resources/health).
